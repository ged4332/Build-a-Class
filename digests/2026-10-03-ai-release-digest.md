## 1. Anthropic

### GLM-5.3 and the spread of advanced cyber capabilities (29 September 2026; not in earlier issues)
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities
What Anthropic says:
- A Frontier Red Team research post examining GLM-5.3, an open-weight model from Zhipu AI, and its capacity to autonomously build end-to-end cyber exploits.
- Anthropic states GLM-5.3 was released without meaningful safeguards to limit misuse. In simulated tests, simple techniques bypassed its safeguards 64 to 100 percent of the time, while the same attacks did not succeed against safeguarded Claude models.
- Anthropic cites NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation finding that GLM-5.3 is the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date, lagging the US frontier by roughly four months on aggregate cyber benchmarks.
- Anthropic frames the dual-use implication plainly: the same lax safeguards that raise capabilities for malicious actors can also help defenders securing their own systems.

### How to use this at Touch Stone Publishers
1. Treat this as board-brief and sector-brief material for the Technology rotation, not an action item. An Anthropic-published, NIST-cited finding that an open-weight competitor model ships without meaningful misuse safeguards is a concrete governance-gap data point for any brief on AI regulation or open-weight model risk.
2. File it alongside Anthropic's own September threat intelligence report as a running thread: competitive safety posture, not just raw capability, is becoming a recurring Anthropic publishing category worth tracking issue to issue.
